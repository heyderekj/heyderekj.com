description: "Marketing site for Harvous—the Bible notes app—redesigned around use cases, product proof, and a clearer path from curious visitor to signup."
brief: |
  Harvous is a notes app for Bible study. The marketing site has to explain a product people often confuse with a Bible reader or a daily devotion—without sounding like either—and get the right people into a free signup.

  I designed and built the current harvous.com end to end: positioning, page system, and the live site that carries use cases, features, a founder letter, blog, pricing, and release notes.
problem: |
  The first Harvous.com was a founder letter with device mockups. It was honest, but thin for visitors who needed to understand *who it’s for*, what ships in the product, and why it isn’t “another Bible app.”

  As the product matured—threads, scripture pills, highlights, recall—the site needed room for proof, pathways by use case, and a publishing surface (blog + release notes) that matches how the product actually ships.
solution: |
  The redesign leads with a sharp line—“Harvous is for people who actually study”—then opens into use-case cards (daily journal, sermon notes, prep, book study) and social proof before the deeper product story.

  Features, compare, pricing, and a personal letter from the founder sit in a shared nav system. Bright Enough (the blog) and Release Notes keep the marketing surface in sync with what ships. Mobile gets the same hierarchy with a compact menu and card stack.
specificWork: |
  - Full redesign of harvous.com: information architecture, visual system, and responsive build
  - Use-case pathways with illustrated cards and testimonial highlights
  - Features narrative (“It all comes together”) with product UI callouts
  - Founder letter page, Bright Enough blog index, and Release Notes grid
  - Mobile nav and use-case browsing patterns
